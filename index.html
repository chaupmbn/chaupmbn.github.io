Abstract

I am an MA student in Economics at the University of Saskatchewan, and I am also completing an MSc in Financial Engineering at WorldQuant University. I work in labour economics and applied econometrics.

My research studies the labour market outcomes of immigrants, the returns to education and retraining, and earnings inequality. Current projects decompose the immigrant–native wage gap in the United States, examine how ethnic enclaves shape the earnings of Vietnamese immigrants, and compare the returns to completing a credential for immigrant and Canadian-born graduates. I also apply machine-learning methods to economic forecasting.

I am a research assistant to Guido Matias Cortes (York University) and Kelly Foley (University of Saskatchewan). Before starting graduate study in economics, I spent more than ten years in hotel management in Vietnam, most recently as Managing Director of Sala Hotel Group.

The Immigrant–Native Wage Gap in the Contemporary United States: An Oaxaca–Blinder and Machine Learning Analysis

with Thanh-Huy Le (Tien Giang University) and Gia Phuc Dao (Simon Fraser University)

This paper measures the wage gap between immigrant and native-born workers in the United States today and decomposes it with the Oaxaca–Blinder method and machine-learning techniques.

  2. Sheepskin Effects for Immigrants and Canadian-Born

    In progressSole-authored MA research project

    Estimates the difference in sheepskin effects between immigrant and Canadian-born students in recent cohorts of postsecondary entrants in Canada: the gap in the earnings return to completing a credential, holding years of schooling constant. Re-examines Ferrer and Riddell (2008) with the Canadian credential directly observed. Proposed data: the Postsecondary Student Information System (PSIS), the T1 Family File (T1FF) and the Longitudinal Immigration Database (IMDB), at a Statistics Canada Research Data Centre.

  3. Weather Impacts on Agricultural Production, Agricultural Commodity Prices and Price Relationships: Evidence from the United States and Europe

    with Minh Tien Phung

    In progressMScFE capstone project, WorldQuant University (proposal stage)

    Code ↗

    Examines how temperature and precipitation affect crop yields, and tests cointegration, error-correction dynamics and the speed of mean reversion in corn–wheat and soybean oil–rapeseed oil price spreads in the United States and Europe.
